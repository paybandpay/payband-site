The EU Pay Transparency Transposition Tracker

27 member states. 1 directive. The deadline has passed and the map is a mess. This tracker shows where each country actually stands, updated as it moves.

Why this matters now

7 June 2026 has been and gone. That was the deadline for every EU member state to transpose the Pay Transparency Directive into national law. Some did. Many did not. The result is a fragmented picture where the rules in Dublin, Madrid and Copenhagen are at different stages, with different local additions.

The next date is the one that bites. Employers with 150 or more employees in an EU member state must file their first gender pay gap reports by 7 June 2027, and those reports are based on 2026 data. Employers with 100 to 149 employees follow by 7 June 2031. The reporting year is already running. If your pay data, job architecture and pay ranges are not in shape now, you cannot fix them retrospectively.

That is why readiness work starts now, not next spring.

How to read it

  • Transposition status for each of the 27 member states: done, draft law, consultation, or nothing yet
  • The local additions that go beyond the directive, where they exist
  • Key national dates, including reporting thresholds and first filing deadlines
  • A changelog, so you can see what moved since you last looked

GR Greece In force 6 July 2026 In force. Law 5316/2026. Core duties bite from 1 November 2026. Fines run to EUR 50,000 with possible business closure. Source
IT Italy In force 7 June 2026 In force. Legislative Decree 96/2026. Pay progression duty applies at 50+ employees; collective agreements frame equal value. Source
LT Lithuania In force 7 June 2026 In force. Sodra calculates pay gaps centrally from monthly employer data. Employer response time is 30 days, not two months. Source
MT Malta In force 7 June 2026 In force. Legal Notice 173 of 2026. No transitional period, and the 2025 recruitment rules were left in place alongside it. Source
SK Slovakia In force 7 June 2026 In force. First member state to transpose. Equal pay claims extend to same gender comparators. Gender neutral pay structures were due 31 July 2026. Source
BE Belgium Partly in force 1 January 2025 Public sector only. Federation Wallonie-Bruxelles decree in force, Flemish public sector from 7 June 2026. No federal private sector bill. Source
EE Estonia Partly in force 13 July 2026 Partly in force. Recruitment transparency only. Reporting and pay structure duties deliberately deferred. Source
PL Poland Partly in force 24 December 2025 Recruitment transparency already in force. Full bill published 4 May 2026, in consultation. Fines PLN 2,000 to 60,000, fixed 31 March notice date. Source
AT Austria Draft law 6 June 2026 Draft in government coordination, not yet before parliament. Stalled by disagreement between the chamber of commerce and the union federation. Source
BG Bulgaria Draft law 19 May 2026 Draft amending the Protection Against Discrimination Act, consultation closed 18 June 2026. Reporting at 150+ from 2027, 100 to 149 from 2031. Source
CY Cyprus Draft law 5 November 2025 Bill published, consultation closed 4 December 2025. Goes beyond the directive by putting explicit weighting of job evaluation criteria in the operative text. Source
CZ Czechia Draft law 16 March 2026 Labour Code amendment published. Pay secrecy clauses already banned since 1 June 2025. Main law targeted for 1 January 2027, reporting from 2028. Source
DK Denmark Draft law 26 February 2026 Bill amending the Equal Pay Act. Gold plated: reporting extends down to 50 to 99 employees via a statistics model, plus a new equal pay institute. Source
FI Finland Draft law 9 July 2026 Government proposal with Parliament, handling from autumn 2026. Reporting threshold 100+ employees. Target in force 1 January 2027. Source
FR France Draft law 4 June 2026 Bill with the Conseil d'Etat. Goes well beyond: 50 employee threshold, fines to 1 percent of payroll, 2 percent for repeats, public sector covered. Source
IE Ireland Draft law January 2025 General Scheme published, main Pay Transparency Bill still unpublished with no timeline. Gender Pay Gap Information Act 2021 already covers 50+ employers. Source
LV Latvia Draft law 26 March 2026 Draft law published, consultation closed 9 April 2026. Requires actual pay figures in job adverts, not ranges, and drops the under 50 exemption. Source
NL Netherlands Draft law 21 May 2026 Bill 36949 before the Tweede Kamer. Target 1 January 2027. Goes further: separate reporting for agency workers, group level reporting allowed. Source
RO Romania Draft law 7 July 2026 Revised draft published by the Senate. Job ads must itemise variable pay and benefits separately. Sanctions set as multiples of minimum wage. Source
SE Sweden Draft law 15 January 2026 Lagradsremiss decided, entry into force pushed to 1 January 2027. Government has said it does not currently intend to submit a bill and wants the directive renegotiated. Source
DE Germany Consultation 7 November 2025 Expert commission reported, no ministerial draft published. Ministry now targets entry into force in early 2027 with first reporting in June 2028. Source
LU Luxembourg Consultation 16 June 2026 Still no bill deposited with the Chamber of Deputies. Minister confirmed the text remains in consultation. Source
SI Slovenia Consultation February 2026 Draft sent to the Economic and Social Council, which had not started substantive work by June 2026. New government must decide whether to keep the draft. Source
ES Spain Consultation 8 May 2026 Prior consultation closed, draft royal decree circulated to social partners in July 2026. Government targets approval before September 2026. Source
HR Croatia No public action Not set Two working groups drafting Labour Act and Gender Equality Act changes. No text released for public consultation. Source
HU Hungary No public action Not set Pay transparency does not appear in any official legislative roadmap. April 2026 elections stalled work further. Source
PT Portugal No public action Not set No draft, consultation or timetable published. Government says transposition is being finalised. Source

Status definitions. In force: national law transposing the directive is in effect. Partly in force: some duties are law, others are not. Draft law: a bill has been published or is before parliament. Consultation: pre-legislative work is under way with no published bill. No public action: no draft, consultation or timetable in the public domain.
